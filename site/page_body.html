Can what young people say to AI chatbots be measured as soundly as their survey answers?

A prototype pipeline that turns donated AI-chat text into daily wellbeing indicators, then puts each indicator through three prespecified validity tests. This page runs the full demo on simulated young adults so you can see every step.

Vasileios Stavropoulos (RMIT University) · Spyridon Kavvadias · prototype v0.2.1 · Code on GitHub

The pipeline

From a chat export to a daily score, in four steps

1 · Parse

Keep only the participant's words

A donated ChatGPT-style export is read and only the participant's own messages are kept, never the AI's replies. Each message is assigned to a study day; messages sent before 04:00 count towards the previous day.

2 · Protect

Remove personal details on the device

Emails, phone numbers, links and self-introduced names are replaced with tags before anything is scored. Only daily scores leave the participant's machine.

3 · Score

Compare meaning with the survey item

Each construct is anchored by its daily survey item (e.g. "I felt lonely") plus paraphrases. A message scores high when it is closer in meaning to those anchors than to ordinary task requests. Scores are averaged per person-day.

4 · Test

Three prespecified validity tests

Convergence with the person's own daily survey answer, robustness to message length and topic words, and agreement with blind human raters. Indicators that fail are reported, not dropped.

Built-in traps

Limits

What this demo shows, and what it does not

The data are simulated from message templates. The results show that the method works end to end and rejects the indicator it should reject; they do not show that real AI conversations carry a valid wellbeing signal. That is the empirical question the method is built to test.

Raters in the demo are simulated from the share of each day's messages that voice the construct, plus rater noise. In a real study, two trained raters code a local-only sample of conversation-days, blind to survey answers and indicators.

The demo encoder only knows the vocabulary of the simulated corpus. Real studies would swap in a pretrained encoder (sentence-transformers or a mental-health language model); comparing encoders on the same three tests is itself a robustness check.

Simulation: 60 young adults × 14 days; states correlated .30; seed 2026. Encoder: offline TF-IDF + SVD.
